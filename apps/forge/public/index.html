Promoter key

Agents may write. Only a person may promote.

  • Each agent signs its work in a fork of its own, with a key that lasts an hour.
  • Main moves only when your passkey approves that exact change, and the approval is stored in the commit.
  • The rules live in the repository, so any clone can check them without this site.

Start a project

The project starts with a small notes repository. Your passkey becomes its only promoter, and approving the first commit makes it the repository's root of trust.

It walks through every step and stops where your passkey is needed.

What happens next

  1. Your passkey signs the first commit. Its hash becomes the identifier of the repository, did:repo: and that hash, the same on every host. The next commit sets the rules, and they name your passkey as the only promoter.
  2. You admit two agents. Each gets an SSH key for an hour and a fork of its own. Changing the rules needs your approval too.
  3. The agents work at the same time. Each signs its commits and proposes them. The forge checks every proposal against the rules.
  4. You promote what you accept. Your passkey approves the exact merge: both parents and the resulting tree.
  5. Anyone checks it anywhere. A fresh clone, a laptop or GitHub gives the same answer, with no forge in the loop.

    Settings

    Passkeys

    Held by is the password manager or security key that stores the passkey, when it can be identified. Synced means that manager can copy the passkey to your other devices. Revoking a passkey stops it signing in, though sessions it already started stay open, and you can't revoke your only one.

    Each of your passkeys is held on one device and can't be copied to another. If you lose that device, only a recovery code gets you back in. Add a passkey on another device or a security key, and keep your recovery codes somewhere safe.

    Recovery codes

    Recovery codes let you add a new passkey if you lose all of yours, and each one works once. Replacing them gives you a fresh set of 8, shown only once, and every old code stops working straight away, including copies kept elsewhere or split into shares.

    Sessions

    A session keeps one browser signed in for 7 days. Log out ends this one, and Log out everywhere ends all of them, this one included. Log out everywhere else keeps this one and asks for your passkey first, so someone holding a stolen session can't shut you out.

    Account details

    Record id:

    Your record id names your identity record and never changes, even if every passkey and code does. It isn't secret. To make yourself an operator on your own deployment, add it to the OPERATOR_RECORD_IDS secret.
